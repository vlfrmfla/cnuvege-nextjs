## Summary

In winter, low root-zone temperature limits root growth in water-culture systems. We grew three lettuce cultivars ('Caipira', 'Volare' and 'Isabel') in a glasshouse in two systems, the nutrient film technique (NFT) and aeroponics. The nutrient solution was heated to 20, 25 or 30°C, and we compared how each setting changed root-zone temperature, root morphology, shoot growth and yield.

## Key Findings

- **Root-zone temperature**: Heating raised root-zone temperature in both systems. The insulated, enclosed Styrofoam aeroponic beds held temperature more steadily than the NFT channels.
- **Root development**: Heated solutions produced longer total root length, more root tips and more lateral roots with a finer average diameter, a root structure better suited to water and nutrient uptake.
- **System comparison**: NFT led in shoot biomass and yield, while aeroponics led in root development and root biomass.
- **Best setting**: Heating to 30°C (NSH30) gave the best growth and yield. Even at this set point, the measured root-zone temperature was only about 25°C, which suited both root and shoot growth of lettuce.
